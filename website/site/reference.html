Principles

The brand idea is Every arrival, precisely composed: hospitality is a sequence of moments, and GEM gives each one its proportion, timing, space and detail. Four principles carry it.

  • Precision over decoration

    If an element does not carry meaning, function or proportion, remove it. A deboss instead of a pattern; a hairline instead of a box; a verified fact instead of an adjective.

  • Restraint is the luxury

    Say less, show less, and let what remains be exact. Empty space is a decision.

  • Geometry is the language

    Ring, arc, quarter and spark are the whole vocabulary. If it is not one of them, it is not a GEM device.

  • Space is part of the identity

    Protect the empty field. Clearspace is not left over; it is designed.

Logo

  • Use the supplied vector kit unchanged: horizontal, stacked, symbol and no-spark symbol, in beige, ink, black and white. Never redraw, trace, stretch, rotate, recolour, outline, add a shadow, crop, mirror or set the wordmark in type.

  • Clearspace unit u is the G-ring radius: 1u minimum, 2u preferred. Minimum digital size: 120 px lockup, 32 px symbol; below 32 px use the no-spark symbol.

  • Beige artwork sits only on Ink or Black. Never place the logo on busy photography or Beige on White.

Typography

Jost 400 · display and headings

A quiet arrival

Inter 400 · body and interface

Inter carries every sentence that must be read, from a booking form to a specification sheet.

  • One weight, 400, until an accepted 500 font file exists. At most two weights in a composition.

  • Tracking only on capitals: tagline 0.18em, eyebrow 0.16em, display capitals 0.08–0.12em. Body text and Arabic are never tracked. Keep lines to 45–75 characters.

  • The tagline is fixed: Hospitality, in perfect proportion, in capitals on one line.

Graphic language

  • Ring

    Completeness. The frame of the G.

  • Arc

    The opening. Welcome and passage.

  • Quarter

    Mass. Weight and anchor.

  • Spark

    The single detail that is noticed. One per composition.

Forms are large, quiet and clear of the words: let the frame crop them, never place them behind text, and never repeat them as a pattern.

Imagery

Ratios: 3:2 for rooms and architecture, 4:5 for products, 16:9 for bands. Light, then subject: editorial and architectural framing, a single light source, warm-neutral tones, controlled contrast and room around the subject. Place the subject on a third and keep text in the quiet area. Avoid generic hotel stock, posed smiling groups, oversaturation, filters, artificial glow and text over busy images. Label every rendered image as a concept.

Voice

Measured, warm, quietly confident and precise. Short sentences. State rather than sell. No exclamation marks and no superlatives without independent evidence.

Label order

Packaging reads from the top down in six levels, in this order. Product and market details are not yet verified.

  1. GEM
  2. Collection or tier
  3. Product and function
  4. Quantity or size
  5. Instructions and market information
  6. SKU and artwork version

Production release

Fourteen steps, none skipped without written approval. Corrections at step 11 return the job to step 5. This is a working standard (Part C, pending production validation).

  1. Brief received
  2. Correct master identified
  3. Technical specification confirmed
  4. Supplier template or dieline
  5. Artwork prepared
  6. Internal preflight
  7. Supplier proof
  8. Brand review
  9. Technical and regulatory review
  10. Physical proof or sample
  11. Corrections
  12. Final approval
  13. Production release
  14. Job record archived

Status labels

Every item says how settled it is, in words and in shape, never by colour alone.

  • Approved

    Final. Use as written. Filled Ink label.

  • Conditional

    Approved, with a stated condition still open. 2px outline.

  • Pending validation

    Evidence or testing outstanding. Never shown as final. Dashed 2px outline.

  • Reference

    Study or inspiration only. Not a GEM standard. Dotted 2px outline.
